# Transplanted Aman Rice (T. Aman) — Cultivation Package & Practices

Source: Bangladesh Agro-Meteorological Information Service (BAMIS), Department of Agricultural Extension, and Bangladesh Rice Research Institute (BRRI)
https://www.bamis.gov.bd/en/croppnp/1/all/74/

Crop: Transplanted Aman (T. Aman) — the main monsoon (Kharif-2) rice crop of Bangladesh.

## Season & Sowing Window
- Season: Kharif-2 (monsoon), mid-July to mid-November.
- Seedbed: raised in late June to mid-July.
- Transplanting window: 15 July to 15 August, using 25-35 day old seedlings. Later transplanting (into September) sharply reduces yield.
- This is the crop to recommend for a "what can I plant now?" question asked in late July / August: unlike Boro (a November-December Rabi crop), Aman's window is open during the monsoon.

## Transplanting
- 2-3 seedlings per hill, spacing about 20-25 cm rows and 15-20 cm hills.
- The crop is mostly rainfed, established into monsoon-wet fields.

## Fertilizer Doses (kg per Bigha; 1 Bigha ≈ 0.33 acre, so multiply by 3 for kg/acre)
| Input | kg/Bigha | kg/Acre (x3) |
|---|---|---|
| Urea | 30 | 90 |
| TSP | 11 | 33 |
| MP (MoP) | 10 | 30 |
| Gypsum | 8 | 24 |
| Zinc Sulphate | 1 | 3 |

Aman needs LESS nitrogen than Boro because monsoon growth is faster and lodging risk is higher with excess urea.

## Fertilizer Application Timing
- TSP, MP, Gypsum and Zinc are applied fully as a basal dose at final land preparation.
- Urea is split into three top dressings: about 15, 35 and 55 days after transplanting (the last one just before panicle initiation).

## Irrigation
- Primarily rainfed. Supplemental irrigation is only needed if the monsoon breaks during establishment (first 20 days).
- The critical period is the reproductive stage (panicle initiation to flowering, roughly day 60-95): a dry spell here causes the biggest yield loss, so keep the field saturated then even if it means pumping.

## Pest Risk
- Yellow stem borer (dead hearts / whiteheads), brown plant hopper (hopperburn) and gall midge (silver-shoot) are the main Aman pests. See the rice pests reference for favorable conditions and treatments; Aman's warm humid monsoon conditions favor stem borer and BPH in particular.

## Harvest & Post-Harvest
- Matures about 120-135 days after transplanting; harvest at 80-85% golden-ripe grain, typically November to early December.
- Harvesting Aman on time frees the land for a Rabi crop — mustard, lentil or Boro rice — which is why an Aman → mustard/lentil → Boro rotation is common.

## Soil Suitability
- Best on clay and clay-loam soils, which hold monsoon rainwater and suit the flooded/saturated regime; well-suited to poorly-drained lowland.
- Sandy and sandy-loam soils drain too fast and need more supplemental irrigation, so they are a weaker fit for rainfed Aman.
