# Onion / Piyaj — Cultivation Package & Practices

Source: Bangladesh Agricultural Research Institute (BARI) spices/vegetable research and Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE) onion package of practices
http://www.bari.gov.bd/
https://www.bamis.gov.bd/en/croppnp/1/all/all/

Crop: Onion (Piyaj), the main bulb spice crop of Bangladesh, grown chiefly in the Rabi (winter) season.

## Varieties
- **BARI Piyaj-1, -4, -5**: improved Rabi bulb varieties.
- **Taherpuri, Faridpuri Bhati**: popular local Rabi types.
- Onion can be grown from seed (transplanted seedlings) or from sets/bulblets; the Rabi seed-to-bulb crop described here gives the highest yield.

## Season & Sowing Window
- Season: Rabi (dry, winter).
- Seedlings are raised in a nursery and transplanted from early November to mid-December; bulbs are harvested in March-April.
- Cool weather during bulbing (December-February) is essential; onions transplanted too late run into heat at bulbing and give small bulbs.

## Seed Rate & Sowing
- About 4 kg/acre of true onion seed to raise seedlings for one acre.
- Onion seed is expensive and short-lived, so use fresh, tested seed each year.
- Transplant 40-45 day old seedlings at roughly 15 cm x 10 cm spacing.

## Fertilizer Doses and Timing (kg/ha; divide by ~2.47 for kg/acre)
- Urea 250, TSP 150, MoP 150, Gypsum 110 kg/ha, plus Zinc and Boron as micronutrients.
- Onion is a heavy feeder and very responsive to SULPHUR — the gypsum dose lifts bulb size and storage quality.
- Timing: apply all TSP, Gypsum, Zinc, Boron and one-third of the urea plus half the MoP as a basal dose. Top-dress the remaining urea in two splits (about 25 and 45 days after transplanting) and the rest of the MoP at the start of bulbing.

## Irrigation
- Onion is shallow-rooted and needs frequent light irrigation.
- Establishment (5-20 days) and bulbing (45-85 days) are the critical periods; keep steady moisture during bulbing but STOP irrigation about 10 days before harvest so the bulbs cure and store well.

## Pest Risk
- **Onion thrips** rasp silvery streaks on the leaves in dry spells and are the major pest; spray Spinosad or Imidacloprid at threshold.
- **Purple blotch (Alternaria porri)** shows purple-centred lesions on leaves in dewy weather; manage with a Mancozeb or Iprodione spray.

## Harvest & Post-Harvest
- Bulbs mature in about 120 days; harvest when the tops fall over ("neck fall") and begin to dry.
- Cure the bulbs in shade for a week before storage; well-cured, sulphur-fed bulbs store far longer, which lets a farmer sell after the March-April price trough.

## Soil Suitability
- Best on well-drained, fertile loam and sandy-loam soils rich in organic matter.
- Avoid heavy waterlogged clay: standing water rots the shallow roots and bulbs, so a low-lying clay field is a poor fit unless drainage is excellent.
