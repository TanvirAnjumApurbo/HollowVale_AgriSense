# Soil Mineralogy, Fertility and Microbial Status in Different AEZs (FRG-2024)

Source: Bangladesh Agricultural Research Council (BARC), Fertilizer Recommendation Guide (FRG) 2024, Section 3 "Soil Mineralogy, Fertility and Microbial Status in Different AEZs"
http://www.barc.gov.bd/

## Agro-ecological zones (AEZs)
Bangladesh has **30 agro-ecological zones**, sub-divided into 88 sub-regions and 535 sub-units. Each AEZ shares agricultural and ecological characteristics defined by four elements:
- **Physiography** — land forms and parent materials.
- **Soils** — texture, water-holding capacity, fertility.
- **Land type** — depth and duration of flooding: highland (HL), medium highland (MHL), medium lowland (MLL), lowland (LL), very lowland (VLL).
- **Agro-climatology** — length of Kharif and Rabi seasons, low-winter-temperature and high-summer-temperature periods.

By area the largest zones are AEZ 29 Northern and Eastern Hills (1.82 mha), AEZ 13 Ganges Tidal Floodplain (1.71 mha) and AEZ 11 High Ganges River Floodplain (1.32 mha). For local fertility data, the respective Upazila Nirdeshika should be consulted, or soil samples analysed.

## National soil fertility status
Bangladesh soils are widely deficient in the major and secondary nutrients (share of arable land):
- **Phosphorus (P)**: very low + low on ~50% of arable land.
- **Sulphur (S)**: very low + low on ~39%.
- **Zinc (Zn)**: very low + low on ~32%.
- **Boron (B)**: very low + low on ~31%.
- **Potassium (K)**: very low + low on ~32%.
- **Organic matter (OM)**: very low + low on ~35%; medium on ~59%.

A healthy soil should hold at least **2.5% organic matter**, but most Bangladesh soils have less than 1.5% and some below 1%. This is the evidence base for treating a typical field as Low-to-Medium fertility and for the strong crop response to P, S, Zn and B.

## Typical fertility by land type
Across floodplain AEZs, soil pH runs about 4.1-6.7 (strongly acidic to near-neutral) and organic matter is low to medium. Nitrogen, phosphorus, zinc and boron are commonly very low to low; sulphur and potassium are often low to medium. Highland and medium-highland soils lose organic matter faster than lowland rice soils.

## Calcareous vs non-calcareous zones
Zinc deficiency is especially common in calcareous soils (AEZs 10, 11, 12, 13) and in wetland rice soils. Magnesium tends to be low in piedmont and Tista floodplain soils. Matching the micronutrient (Zn, B, Mg) doses to the zone is what separates a good fertilizer plan from a generic one.

## Fragile ecosystems (Delta Plan 2100 hotspots)
Five ecologically challenged regions have generally low fertility and low cropping intensity:
- **Haor and flash-flood areas** — Sylhet and Mymensingh region.
- **Char ecosystem** (rivers and estuaries) — Rangpur and Dhaka region.
- **Barind and drought-prone areas** — Rajshahi region.
- **Coastal zones** (salinity) — Khulna and Barishal region.
- **Chittagong Hill Tracts** — Chattogram region.

The Barind tract shows the lowest available sulphur and generally low organic matter; coastal soils carry salinity and variable pH; haor basins are acidic and flood-prone. A season plan should account for these constraints, not just the crop.

## Soil microbial status
Soils across the AEZs carry populations of total bacteria, free-living N2-fixing bacteria, phosphate-solubilising bacteria, Rhizobium/Bradyrhizobium, fungi and actinomycetes (10^4 to 10^9 colony-forming units per gram). These microbes drive nitrogen fixation, phosphate solubilisation and organic-matter mineralisation, and are why organic-matter management and biofertilisers pay off over time.
