# Fertilizers and Their Use in Bangladesh (FRG-2024)

Source: Bangladesh Agricultural Research Council (BARC), Fertilizer Recommendation Guide (FRG) 2024, Section 4 "Fertilizers and Their Use in Bangladesh"
http://www.barc.gov.bd/

## Types of fertilizer
- **Inorganic (mineral) fertilizer**: nutrients as inorganic salts, e.g. TSP, MoP, gypsum.
- **Organic fertilizer**: carbonaceous plant/animal material, e.g. cowdung, poultry manure, compost.
- **Straight fertilizer**: only one primary nutrient (N, P or K), e.g. urea, TSP, MoP.
- **Compound fertilizer**: two or more nutrients, e.g. DAP (18% N, 20% P).
- **Micronutrient fertilizer**: Zn, B, Fe, Mn, Cu, Mo — needed in small amounts, e.g. zinc sulphate, boric acid.
- **Coated/slow-release fertilizer**: e.g. neem-coated urea. **Granular**: USG, UMG.
- **Biofertilizer**: living beneficial microbes (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Azospirillum) that fix N or solubilise P.
- **Liming material**: raises soil pH; dololime [CaMg(CO3)2] is common in Bangladesh.

## Deep placement of urea (USG / UMG)
Urea Super Granules (0.9-1.8 g) and Urea Mega Granules (2.7 g) are pressed from ordinary urea and placed 8-10 cm deep at the centre of four rice hills, one week after transplanting. Deep placement raises nitrogen-use efficiency by cutting ammonia volatilisation, denitrification, leaching and runoff. It saves about **35% of urea-N in rice** and about 20% in vegetable and fruit crops (cabbage, cauliflower, tomato, potato, papaya).

## Biofertilizers
- **Rhizobial inoculant** fixes atmospheric nitrogen in symbiosis with legumes (lentil, chickpea, mungbean, blackgram, groundnut, soybean). Coat seed with molasses + peat-based inoculant, dry in shade (never direct sun), and sow immediately. When Rhizobium is used, nitrogen fertilizer is not applied to the pulse.
- **Azolla** is a floating fern that fixes N in wetland rice, contributing 25-40 kg N/ha; apply to standing water 1-2 weeks after transplanting.

## Time and method of application
Fertilizer-use efficiency depends heavily on timing and method — as much as 70% of applied urea can be lost under wetland rice. Three methods: **broadcast** (common in field crops), **localized/band placement** (common in horticulture), and **foliar spray** (useful only for micronutrients, never for macronutrients).

Key rules:
- Do not apply fertilizer too close to seeds or young roots, or to wet young leaves.
- Urea should not be applied to standing water; apply manure 7-10 days before sowing.
- **Rice (except broadcast Aman)**: apply urea in three equal splits — final land preparation, maximum tillering, and 5-7 days before panicle initiation.
- **Rabi crops without irrigation**: apply the full urea dose as basal at final land preparation. With irrigation, split urea in three.
- Phosphate 1-2 days before final land preparation; potassium, sulphur, magnesium and boron as full basal dose. In coarse/light soils, split potassium half basal and half at tillering.
- Zinc and phosphate should not be mixed during storage.

## Residual effect — why later crops need less
Except nitrogen, most nutrients leave a residual effect for the next crop, so applying full rates to every crop is uneconomical.
- More than 60% of applied N is lost, so **urea is applied at full rate to every crop**.
- Phosphorus uptake is only 15-25% of what is applied; the P rate for a second/third crop can drop 40-50% for rice and jute, 30-40% for vegetables.
- Potassium after potato, sugarcane or vegetables can drop 30-40% for the following crop.
- Under rainfed (no-irrigation) conditions, reduce all recommended nutrients by 25%; expect yield loss of ~15% for rice and jute, ~20% for potato, ~35% for wheat, oilseed and vegetables.

## Crop response to soil-test value
Soil-test values classify each nutrient into five categories (very low to very high). The probability of a profitable yield response **rises as the soil-test value falls** — a low soil test means a strong response to that nutrient, which is why AgriSense treats most Bangladesh soils as Low-to-Medium fertility and doses accordingly.

## 4R stewardship
Best-practice fertilizer management follows the 4Rs: **right source, right rate, right time, right place.**
