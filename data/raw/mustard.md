# Mustard / Sarisha (Rapeseed-Mustard) — Cultivation Package & Practices

Source: Bangladesh Agricultural Research Institute (BARI) oilseed research and Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE) / BAMIS mustard package of practices
http://www.bari.gov.bd/
https://www.bamis.gov.bd/en/croppnp/1/all/all/

Crop: Mustard (Sarisha), the main Rabi oilseed of Bangladesh.

## Varieties
- **BARI Sarisha-14 / -15 / -17**: improved short-duration varieties, ~80-90 days.
- **Tori-7**: extra-early (~75-80 days), often grown as a catch crop between T. Aman and Boro rice.

## Season & Sowing Window
- Season: Rabi (dry, winter).
- Sowing window: mid-October to mid-November, right after the Aman rice harvest.
- Its short 80-90 day duration is the key advantage: mustard fits neatly between Aman (harvested Nov-Dec) and Boro (transplanted Nov-Dec), so a farmer can take an extra oilseed harvest without missing the Boro window.

## Seed Rate & Sowing
- Seed rate: about 7-8 kg/ha (~3 kg/acre), broadcast or line-sown.
- Sowing depth 2-3 cm into moist, well-tilled soil.

## Fertilizer Doses and Timing (kg/ha; divide by ~2.47 for kg/acre)
- Urea: 250-300 kg/ha, TSP: 170-200 kg/ha, MoP: 85-100 kg/ha, Gypsum: 150-180 kg/ha, plus Boron and Zinc as micronutrients.
- Mustard is very responsive to SULPHUR — the gypsum dose is important and directly lifts oil content and yield; skipping it is a common mistake.
- Timing: apply all TSP, MoP, Gypsum and about two-thirds of the urea as a basal dose at final land preparation. Top-dress the remaining urea before flowering (around 25-30 days after sowing), ideally with the first irrigation.

## Irrigation
- Needs only 1-2 light irrigations: one at the vegetative stage (20-25 days after sowing) and one at flowering / early pod stage (45-50 days).
- Water stress at flowering causes the sharpest yield loss, so prioritise the flowering irrigation if water is limited.

## Pest Risk
- **Mustard aphid** is the major pest, colonising flowering shoots and pods; scout from flowering onward and spray Imidacloprid or Malathion at first colonies.
- **Alternaria blight** causes concentric dark leaf and pod spots in dewy weather; manage with an Iprodione or Mancozeb spray.

## Harvest & Post-Harvest
- Matures in 80-90 days; harvest when the lower pods turn yellow-brown but before they shatter and drop seed (usually January to mid-February).
- Cut in the cool morning to reduce shattering losses, then dry and thresh.

## Soil Suitability
- Best on well-drained loam and sandy-loam soils.
- Avoid heavy, waterlogged clay: mustard roots do not tolerate standing water, so a low-lying clay field that holds moisture is a poor fit unless drainage is good.
