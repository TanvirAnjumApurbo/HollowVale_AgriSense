# Bangladesh Soil Types and Cropping Seasons — Reference Notes

## Soil Types
Source: Banglapedia, "Bangladesh Soil"
https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Bangladesh_Soil

- **Floodplain soils** cover about 79% of Bangladesh's total area. Textures range from sandy and silty alluvium, through silt loam and silty clay loam on riverbanks, to silty clays in low-lying basins. Grey and dark grey floodplain soils are common. Drainage ranges from well/excessively drained (higher land) to poorly drained (basin/lowland areas).
- **Hill soils** cover about 12.7% of the area, textured sandy loam to sandy or silty clay loam, and are strongly to extremely acidic. Concentrated in northern and eastern hill regions.
- **Terrace soils** cover about 8.4% of the area, red-brown or grey, typically friable clay or silty in texture.
- Calcareous alluvial soils are found in the Ganges floodplain; non-calcareous types dominate the Tista, Karatoya-Bangali, Jamuna, middle Meghna, and eastern Surma-Kushiyara floodplains.

Source: general Bangladesh Agro-Ecological Zone (AEZ) references (Banglapedia "Agroecological Zone"; SRDI)
- Bangladesh is divided into 30 Agro-Ecological Zones (AEZs), further subdivided into 88 sub-regions and 535 agroecological units, based on soil, weather, water, and land topography (highland / medium land / lowland).
- AEZ 12 and 13 are mostly clay soils. AEZ 4, 8, 9, 11, 25 and 28 have clay loam soils. AEZ 22, 23 and 29 are mostly sandy-textured.
- The Soil Resources Development Institute (SRDI) has identified about 500 soil series in Bangladesh and is the authoritative source for local soil test data.

### Practical implications for crop choice
- Sandy / sandy loam soils drain quickly and hold less water — better suited to crops with lower continuous water needs (e.g. lentil, wheat) unless irrigation is reliable; risk of nutrient leaching, so split fertilizer doses matter more.
- Clay / clay loam soils hold water and nutrients well and suit flood-tolerant crops like Boro/Aman rice, but are slower to drain, which is a risk for crops that dislike waterlogging (e.g. potato, lentil).
- Loam soils are generally the most versatile and suit the widest range of crops including maize and potato.

## Cropping Seasons in Bangladesh
Source: Bangladesh Agro-Meteorological Information Service (BAMIS) / Department of Agricultural Extension; Banglapedia "Agricultural Calendar"

- **Kharif-1**: mid-March to mid-July. Major crop: Aus rice.
- **Kharif-2**: mid-July to mid-November. Major crops: Transplanted Aman (T. Aman) and Broadcast Aman (B. Aman) rice.
- **Rabi**: mid-November to mid-March. Major crops: Boro rice, wheat, potato, mustard, lentil.

This means: rice (Aus, Aman) spans the monsoon Kharif seasons, while Boro rice, wheat, potato, and lentil are Rabi (dry season, largely irrigation- or residual-moisture-dependent) crops. Maize is grown in both Rabi (mid-Oct to mid-Nov sowing) and Kharif-1 (mid-Feb to mid-Mar sowing) windows. Jute is sown in Kharif-1 (April to early May).
