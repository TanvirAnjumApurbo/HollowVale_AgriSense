# Chickpea / Chola (Bengal Gram) — Cultivation Package & Practices

Source: Bangladesh Agricultural Research Institute (BARI) pulses research and Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE) chickpea package of practices
http://www.bari.gov.bd/
https://www.bamis.gov.bd/en/croppnp/1/all/all/

Crop: Chickpea (Chola / Bengal gram), a Rabi pulse grown on residual soil moisture, valued as a protein source and for fixing nitrogen in the soil.

## Varieties
- **BARI Chola-5, -8, -10**: improved, higher-yielding, wilt-tolerant varieties.
- Desi (small, dark-seeded) types dominate in Bangladesh.

## Season & Sowing Window
- Season: Rabi (dry, winter).
- Sowing window: late October to end of November, sown into the moisture left after the Aman rice harvest.
- Like lentil, it is largely a residual-moisture crop and needs little or no irrigation, which makes it cheap to grow.

## Seed Rate & Sowing
- About 16 kg/acre (~40 kg/ha), line-sown or broadcast into well-drained soil.
- Treat seed with Rhizobium inoculant where available to boost nitrogen fixation.

## Fertilizer Doses and Timing (kg/ha; divide by ~2.47 for kg/acre)
- As a legume, chickpea fixes most of its own nitrogen, so only a small starter dose is needed.
- Approximate single basal dose at sowing: Urea 40, TSP 80, MoP 40, Gypsum 50 kg/ha — apply it all at sowing, not as top-dressing.
- The sulphur in gypsum benefits pod filling.

## Irrigation
- Usually grown rainfed on residual moisture.
- If the soil dries out badly, one light irrigation at the pre-flowering stage (30-45 days) protects yield; avoid heavy irrigation, which causes excessive vegetative growth and disease.

## Pest Risk
- **Pod borer (Helicoverpa)** is the major pest, with larvae boring into developing pods; use pheromone traps and spray Emamectin benzoate at flowering/early podding.
- **Botrytis grey mould** appears as grey fungal growth on shoots and pods in a humid, dense canopy; manage with a Carbendazim spray and wider spacing for airflow.

## Harvest & Post-Harvest
- Matures in about 120 days; harvest when the plants yellow and the pods dry, before they shatter.
- Dry the seed well before storage to prevent bruchid (storage weevil) damage.

## Soil Suitability
- Best on well-drained loam and clay-loam soils; tolerates lighter sandy-loam.
- Dislikes waterlogging — standing water causes root rot and wilt, so a low-lying clay field that holds water is a poor fit.
