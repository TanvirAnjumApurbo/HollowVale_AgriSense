# Potato — Fertilizer Management in Bangladesh

Source: "Fertilizer management practices in potato cultivation: a baseline study for the introduction of GE potato in Bangladesh", PMC (National Center for Biotechnology Information)
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272610/

## Recommended Fertilizer Doses (baseline/official recommendation referenced by the study)
| Nutrient/input | Dose |
|---|---|
| Urea (N) | 250 kg/ha |
| TSP (P) | 150 kg/ha |
| MoP (K) | 250 kg/ha |
| Gypsum (S) | 120 kg/ha |
| Zinc sulfate | 10 kg/ha |
| Magnesium sulfate | 100 kg/ha |
| Boric acid | 10 kg/ha |
| Cow dung | 9,071 kg/ha |

## Application Timing
- Urea and MoP are applied as split doses at the first and second earthing-up.
- TSP (P), Magnesium (Mg), Gypsum (S), and Boron (B) are applied as basal doses during final soil preparation.

## Notes on Real-World Practice
The underlying study (surveying four potato-growing regions of Bangladesh, 2020-2021) found many farmers apply nitrogen well above the recommended dose (up to ~134% of recommendation in the Central/Mid-East region), which is a caution for over-fertilization risk, not a recommendation to exceed the table above.

## Season & Water Need
Potato is a Rabi (dry season, roughly November-April) crop in Bangladesh. Water need is medium — it needs consistent moisture especially during tuber bulking but does not tolerate waterlogging.

## Sowing Window
Seed tubers are planted from early November to mid-December, once temperatures cool. Seed rate is high — about 1.5 tonnes per hectare of seed tuber.

## Irrigation
Potato needs 3-5 light irrigations, most critically through tuber bulking (about day 40-70). Keep the soil consistently moist but never waterlogged — standing water triggers tuber rot and late blight. Stop irrigation about 10 days before harvest to firm the tuber skins.

## Pest & Disease Risk
Late blight is the major potato disease in Bangladesh — water-soaked leaf lesions with a pale margin that spread fast in cool, foggy weather; spray preventively with Mancozeb and switch to Cymoxanil+Mancozeb once symptoms appear. Cutworms cut young stems at the soil line early in the season.

## Harvest & Post-Harvest
Potato matures about 90-100 days after planting; harvest when the haulm (top growth) yellows and dies back, usually February-March. Cure the tubers in shade for a few days before storage or move them to cold storage to reduce rotting.

## Soil Suitability
Best on friable, well-drained loam and sandy-loam soils that let the tubers expand. Heavy clay is a poor fit — it waterlogs, deforms tubers and raises rot and blight risk, which is why potato scores low on clay soils.
