# Climate-Smart Soil Management Practices (FRG-2024)

Source: Bangladesh Agricultural Research Council (BARC), Fertilizer Recommendation Guide (FRG) 2024, Section 5 "Climate Smart Soil Management Practices"
http://www.barc.gov.bd/

## Identifying adulterated fertilizer
A standard **organic fertilizer** (per the 02 April 2008 Ministry of Agriculture gazette) should be non-granular, dark grey to black, odourless and free of bad smell, and should not form a clod when pressed in the hand. These physical checks give a preliminary idea of quality, but certainty needs laboratory chemical analysis. Adulterated mineral fertilizers (urea, DAP, MoP, ZnSO4) can also be screened by comparing the colour and clarity of their water solutions against a known good sample.

## Soil organic matter management
Soil organic matter is the "life force of the soil" — it improves structure, water-holding capacity and aeration, stores N, P and S, and feeds beneficial organisms (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, earthworms). Bangladesh soils are chronically low: most below 1.5% organic matter versus the 2.5% a good soil should hold. Replenish it through:
- **Crop residues** — recycle roots, straw and stalks by composting, mulching or direct incorporation instead of burning.
- **Animal manure** — cowdung and poultry manure; store in a shaded pit and keep moist to cut nitrogen loss. Never apply fresh manure to a standing crop.
- **Compost** — layered plant/animal waste, 4-6 months to mature; adding urea and TSP speeds rotting of slow materials like straw and rice husk. Earthworm composting gives **vermicompost**; Trichoderma-accelerated composting gives trichocompost.
- **Bio-slurry** — the digestate left after biogas extraction, a good organic source that keeps the fertilizer value of the original manure.
- **Green manure** — legumes like dhaincha (Sesbania), sunhemp, cowpea ploughed down at 50-55 days old add 10-15 t/ha biomass and 60-120 kg N/ha. Azolla serves the same role in Boro rice fields.

## Integrated Nutrient Management (INM)
INM combines mineral fertilizer with organic manure, crop residues, compost, N-fixing crops and biofertilisers, managed across the whole cropping pattern rather than a single crop. Neither fertilizer nor manure alone can sustain fertility and yield over time; their combination can. The fertilizer requirement is: **A = B − C**, where A is the amount to supply from fertilizer, B is the crop's total nutrient need (by soil test or AEZ), and C is the nutrient supplied by manure. Example (potato): of a 135 kg N/ha need, 5 t/ha cowdung supplies ~30 kg N, so ~105 kg N/ha comes from fertilizer.

## Biochar
Biochar is a carbon-rich product of pyrolysing waste biomass (rice husk, straw, bagasse) under low-oxygen, low-temperature conditions. It sequesters carbon, raises water-holding capacity and CEC, reduces soil acidity, and adds N, P and K — most useful on light-textured, low-organic-matter soils. It is still at the research stage in Bangladesh and not commercially available; annual addition should not exceed 2 t/ha to protect beneficial soil microbes.

## Liming
Liming corrects soil acidity, removes the toxic effect of aluminium, iron and manganese, and improves availability of phosphorus, molybdenum, calcium and magnesium. It is mainly for dryland crops (maize, wheat, grain legumes, oilseeds). Target soil pH is 6.5-7.0. Apply **dololime at 1-2 t/ha to soils with pH below 5.5**, in the dry season 3-4 weeks before the next crop; the effect lasts about three years. Wood ash, press mud and paper-mill lime sludge also work as liming materials.

## Conservation and organic agriculture
**Conservation Agriculture** rests on three pillars — minimum tillage (zero/strip/bed planting), crop-residue retention, and legume-based crop rotation — cutting fuel cost, soil disturbance and greenhouse-gas emissions while raising nitrogen-use efficiency. Its benefits often take about five years to appear and it can raise herbicide reliance. **Organic agriculture** avoids synthetic pesticides, fertilizers and GMOs, relying on rotations, residues, manures, legumes and biological pest control; it suits homestead gardens and low-input cereals better than pest-prone fruit and vegetables.
