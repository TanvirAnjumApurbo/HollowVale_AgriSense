# Chili / Morich — Cultivation Package & Practices

Source: Bangladesh Agricultural Research Institute (BARI) spices research and Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE) chili package of practices
http://www.bari.gov.bd/
https://www.bamis.gov.bd/en/croppnp/1/all/all/

Crop: Chili (Morich), a high-value spice/vegetable grown mainly as a winter (Rabi) transplanted crop, and also in summer.

## Varieties
- **BARI Morich-1, -2, -3**: improved varieties for higher yield and disease tolerance.
- Many local hot-pepper types are grown; hybrid seed is common for green-chili market production.

## Season & Sowing Window
- Season: Rabi (winter) is the main crop; seedlings are transplanted from mid-September to mid-November.
- It is a long-duration crop (about 150 days to the end of picking) with repeated harvests of green chili, so it occupies the field well into the following spring.

## Seed Rate & Sowing
- About 0.4 kg/acre of seed raised as seedlings in a nursery.
- Transplant 30-35 day old seedlings at roughly 50 cm x 40 cm spacing on raised beds for drainage.

## Fertilizer Doses and Timing (kg/ha; divide by ~2.47 for kg/acre)
- Urea 200, TSP 150, MoP 120, Gypsum 100 kg/ha, plus Zinc and Boron.
- Timing: apply all TSP, Gypsum, Zinc, Boron and one-third of the urea with half the MoP as a basal dose. Top-dress the remaining urea in two splits (about 25 and 50 days after transplanting) with the rest of the MoP at flowering.
- Balanced potash and boron improve fruit set and reduce flower drop.

## Irrigation
- Keep the young crop moist through establishment (5-25 days).
- During flowering and fruiting (50-120 days) avoid BOTH water stress and waterlogging — irregular moisture causes flower and fruit drop, while standing water invites root and fruit rots.

## Pest Risk
- **Thrips and mites** cause upward leaf curl (thrips) and downward cupping with bronzing (mites); this "leaf curl complex" is the main chili problem — spray Abamectin or Spiromesifen at threshold.
- **Anthracnose / fruit rot (Colletotrichum)** makes sunken dark spots with concentric rings on ripening fruit in wet weather; spray Azoxystrobin or Mancozeb and pick affected fruit out.

## Harvest & Post-Harvest
- Green chili picking starts about 80 days after transplanting and continues in repeated rounds; leaving fruit to ripen red for dry chili lowers total green yield but fetches a different market.
- Pick in the cool morning and market quickly, as green chili is highly perishable.

## Soil Suitability
- Best on well-drained fertile loam and sandy-loam soils.
- Heavy clay that holds water is a poor fit — chili is very sensitive to waterlogging and root disease, so use raised beds and good drainage on heavier soils.
